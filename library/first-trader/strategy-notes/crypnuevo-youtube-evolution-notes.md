# CrypNuevo YouTube Evolution Notes

## Scope

This file tracks how CrypNuevo's strategy appears to evolve across YouTube videos, starting from the oldest uploads.

## Batch 001: April 17-21, 2023

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-001-oldest-videos-2023-04-17-to-2023-04-21.md`

### Baseline Framework

The earliest videos already show a mature liquidity framework:

- Sunday Twitter update as weekly roadmap.
- Daily YouTube follow-ups to update scenarios.
- BTC levels from ranges, support/resistance, wick imbalances, and liquidation heatmaps.
- DXY and SPX as context.
- Asia/London/New York session behavior.
- Stop-hunt / liquidity-run interpretation.
- Invalidation if the expected session or level behavior does not appear.
- Weekend caution because of lower liquidity and traps.

### Early Repeated Process

1. Identify the main BTC range or support/resistance zone.
2. Check whether liquidity sits at that level.
3. Check DXY and sometimes SPX for directional pressure.
4. Watch the session that may deliver the move.
5. If a level fails, shift to the next zone rather than fighting price.
6. Avoid bias and trade level by level.

### Early Change Baseline

No evolution is visible yet because this is the starting point. Future batches should be measured against this baseline:

- Did he add new tools?
- Did weekly updates become more structured?
- Did wick-fill become more central?
- Did he move from Hyblock-only liquidity toward other heatmaps/Bookmap/indicators?
- Did he formalize execution triggers and invalidation more clearly?

## Batch 002: April 22-26, 2023

## Batch 003: April 27-May 16, 2023

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-003-videos-011-to-025-2023-04-27-to-2023-05-16.md`

### Evolution

The system broadens from BTC daily follow-ups into a fuller market framework:

- trade imbalances left by large candles;
- use FOMC/CPI as risk filters;
- treat daily 50EMA loss as a major structure change but allow fakeouts;
- prepare altcoin charts using HTF ranges and imbalances;
- use BTC dominance to judge whether altcoins can outperform or make new lows.

The core edge is still liquidity and market psychology. The new development is a wider context stack: macro calendar, BTC.D, and altcoin high-timeframe mapping.

## Batch 005: June 8-July 18, 2023

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-005-videos-041-to-055-2023-06-08-to-2023-07-18.md`
- `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-videos-026-050-trade-collection.md`

### Evolution

The range-deviation strategy becomes much more exact:

- define range high, range low, mid-range, and range quarters;
- identify liquidity outside the range;
- wait for a sweep/deviation outside the range;
- require return inside the range and consolidation before execution;
- target mid-range first, then opposite-side liquidity or a breakout through the opposite edge.

CPI/FOMC trading also becomes more procedural. He does not want to guess the data; he wants to wait 30-45 minutes, expect a false first move, and then trade only if price gives a reclaim/retest or a clean deviation setup.

This batch also adds a useful false-breakout filter: if a breakout candle into resistance has weak volume and quickly returns inside the range, he treats it as manipulation/liquidity engineering rather than healthy continuation.

## Batch 006: July 19-August 11, 2023

Evidence:

### Evolution

The workflow becomes explicitly cross-market:

## Batch 007: August 16-September 15, 2023

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-007-videos-071-to-085-2023-08-16-to-2023-09-15.md`
- `sources/first-trader/videos/chart-frames/batch-007-videos-071-085/`

### Evolution

This is the first batch analyzed with sampled chart frames. The visual evidence confirms a repeated chart-planning language:

- turquoise/blue boxes for support, liquidity, and buy/watch zones;
- white horizontal range levels for high, low, mid-range, and prior support/resistance;
- white/yellow path projections showing sweep -> reclaim -> mid-range/EMA target -> possible rejection;
- circles around traps, wick fills, or exact retest points;
- Hyblock/order-book checks after the TradingView path is drawn.

The strategy evolves into a route map rather than a single target. He often draws the whole route: false move, support sweep, reclaim, mid-range target, possible rejection, and later HTF target.

News is treated as catalyst, not thesis. In the ETF/Grayscale sequence, he argues the short-squeeze setup existed before the news; the news only accelerated a technical move that was already prepared by liquidity and trapped positioning.

## Batch 008: September 18, 2023-February 13, 2024

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-008-videos-086-to-100-2023-09-18-to-2024-02-13.md`
- `sources/first-trader/videos/chart-frames/batch-008-videos-086-100/`

### Evolution

The strategy separates clearly by time horizon:

- Sunday/weekly update: broad route and swing map.
- Day trade: session timing, wick, EMA, and smaller liquidity target.
- News day: wait for FOMC/CPI volatility to settle, then trade imbalances.
- Higher-timeframe bullish target: valid only after lower-timeframe fakeout risk is resolved.

Three explicit patterns are now part of the playbook:

- 100% wick fill becomes likely only if price holds/consolidates after the 50% fill instead of rejecting.
- Manipulation trade requires sweep -> return below/above level -> retest as resistance/support, not blind limit orders.
- Market maker changing to shorts is inferred after vertical pump, upside liquidity taken, and stair-step consolidation/distribution boxes appear.

## Batch 009: February 14-March 28, 2024

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-009-videos-101-to-115-2024-02-14-to-2024-03-28.md`
- `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-videos-101-115-trade-chart-patterns.md`
- `sources/first-trader/videos/chart-frames/batch-009-videos-101-115/`

### Evolution

This batch turns several ideas from prior videos into stricter execution rules.

The stop-loss-zone model becomes explicit. He marks obvious retail resistance/support, explains where shorts/longs likely place stops, then waits for the market maker sweep. A sweep is not enough by itself; the trade comes from the reaction after the sweep, such as return below resistance, retest failure, reclaim, or support hold.

The 4h50EMA becomes a hard gate rather than a loose moving average:

- for a short, he wants a close below the 4h50EMA and two 4h candles testing it as resistance;
- if price reclaims before that two-candle test completes, the short is cancelled;
- if price reclaims and holds the 4h50EMA as support, upside continuation becomes more likely.

Video 106 adds M-pattern structure as a liquidity/distribution pattern, not a standalone shape. He wants failed acceptance above a range or high, EMA/range loss, and a nearby liquidity target.

Video 110 adds momentum as a timing filter. Even after a valid target or sweep, he waits for momentum to cool before fading. If momentum stays strong after a reclaim, he prefers continuation toward the next liquidity pocket.

Videos 108 and 111 add a conditional altcoin rule: BTC.D fade matters only if BTC is stable/up and alts have room to catch up. Video 113 separately shows narrative/sector selection for AI tokens, which is different from the BTC day-trading liquidity map.

## Batch 010: April 1-December 7, 2024

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-010-videos-116-to-130-2024-04-01-to-2024-12-07.md`
- `sources/first-trader/videos/chart-frames/batch-010-videos-116-130/`

### Evolution

The channel format changes into deeper market-outlook work. Instead of only daily setup updates, he now often starts on weekly/daily structure, marks major range highs/lows, then works down into 4h/1h triggers. This preserves the old playbook but makes the timeframe hierarchy clearer.

Video 122 confirms the core system by showing what he put into the TradingView indicator:

- wick-fill strategy with 50% and 100% levels;
- liquidity-pool marking;
- trading sessions;
- multi-timeframe 50MA.

The indicator is used as context support, not as an entry signal. The entry still needs support/reclaim, rejection, EMA behavior, or range acceptance.

Videos 123-125 refine swing vs day-trade separation. A move can be valid for a day trade while still not being enough for a swing. Swing longs need reclaim of larger levels such as range lows/highs, daily 50MA, or major HTF support.

Video 126 adds spot buying as a separate framework: BTC near HTF/range support, ETH/alts showing relative strength, BTC.D not pressuring alts, and a clear invalidation. Video 129 extends this into altseason logic using BTC, BTC.D, TOTAL3, ETH, and individual alt charts.

Video 130 returns to long-wick analysis in a larger market-outlook setting: 50% wick fill is the practical first target, while 100% wick fill requires continued support/reclaim and no hard rejection.

## Batch 011: December 11, 2024-March 11, 2025

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-011-videos-131-to-137-2024-12-11-to-2025-03-11.md`

### Evolution

The system's *execution* and *risk* layer becomes explicit for the first time. Wick fills are managed manually (no hard stop in the fill zone; consolidation at the 50% level signals continuation to the 100%). He shows concrete staged sizing (reserve size per level; deploy the final tranche only on acceptance above resistance; compound winners rather than average losers), an order-book spoof check (read only near the level; vanishing walls = spoof), and range sub-structure (range quarters; observe the rejection instead of placing short limits at range highs). The weekly 50MA is promoted to the explicit bull/bear regime line, with the bear trigger being a weekly close below it followed by a retest as resistance. He openly admits a wrong BTC.D top call, reinforcing the rebuild-on-invalidation trait.

## Batch 012: November 3, 2025-January 20, 2026 (post-hiatus, current)

Evidence:

- `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-012-videos-138-to-151-2025-11-03-to-2026-01-20.md`

### Evolution

After an ~8-month 2025 hiatus, the channel returns with his most mechanically detailed and rationale-driven phase. He explains *why* liquidity hunting works (exchange-owned market makers with access to trader stops/liquidations; cites the FTX/Alameda ruling) and adds the concepts "room for liquidity / liquidity run," "liquidity trend lines," and cross-exchange wick validation. New tools enter the stack as context-only: options gamma / gamma-flush, Hyblock liquidation data (delta >20-25B = squeeze signal), the M2 global liquidity index, and Bitcoin mining cost as a floor. Execution is fully spelled out: laddered limit entries, take-profits preset at entry (66/33 or 1/3-2/3), breakeven stops, news-aware flat-into-data, no longs under HTF resistance, no shorts against momentum, and flat at mid-range. Macro stance: bullish 2026 on a liquidity thesis, with an explicit rejection of the four-year-cycle and seasonality in favor of price action. Cross-asset: XRP/DOGE overpump as a reversal tell; alts wait for BTC. He also reveals a long-term investor side (copper/metals/real estate) and a Telegram-first, scam-aware, humility-heavy public voice.
