## Strategy Map

### Market Structure

- Always identify whether price is trending, ranging, consolidating, deviating, reclaiming, or retesting an important EMA.

### Liquidity

- Mark obvious liquidity above and below current price before discussing direction: long wicks, range highs/lows, liquidation clusters, prior highs/lows, OI gaps, CME gaps, and Bookmap clusters.

### Support/Resistance or Supply/Demand

- Identify key reaction zones and explain whether price is accepting, rejecting, deviating, or returning inside the range.

### Trend and Range Conditions

- In a range or consolidation, treat liquidation targets as magnets and focus on extremes before midpoint noise.
- In a mature range, mark range high, range low, mid-range, and quarters; use quarters as support/resistance checkpoints and continuation targets.
- Treat mid-range as a magnet after a valid range-low reclaim or support-box sweep.
- At an obvious support/resistance level, infer where retail stops are clustered before deciding direction; the sweep and reaction matter more than the level touch itself.

### Higher-Timeframe Bias

- Ask for or infer higher-timeframe context before judging a lower-timeframe entry; pay special attention to 1W50EMA as bull/bear context when available.
- Use DXY and SPX as contextual pressure gauges when evidence is available; early YouTube videos repeatedly check DXY inverse pressure and SPX risk context.
- Use DXY route as a filter, not an automatic signal: DXY reclaim/hold pressures BTC down; DXY failed reclaim/deviation can support BTC recovery, but BTC structure and liquidity still decide.
- For altseason or spot-buying analysis, include BTC trend/support, BTC dominance, TOTAL3/altcoin market cap, ETH relative strength, and individual altcoin structure.

### Lower-Timeframe Execution

- Lower-timeframe triggers should support the higher-timeframe idea and must be defined before entry.
- For 4h50EMA shorts, videos 101-115 add a strict rule: close below the 4h50EMA, then see two 4h candles test it as resistance before treating downside as high probability.
- For 4h50EMA longs, reclaim and support test can cancel the short and reopen upside continuation.
- Do not mix day-trade and swing-trade triggers: a bounce into resistance can be a day trade while still failing the conditions for a swing long.

### Invalidation

- Every trade idea needs a specific invalidation level or condition; do not treat a level touch as enough.
- A planned entry can be cancelled before triggering if a new wick, gap, news shock, or acceptance through the level changes the thesis.
- If a plan is invalidated, rebuild from a clean chart instead of defending the old projection.

### Risk/Reward

- Targets usually come from liquidity pools, wick fills, range extremes, prior highs/lows, or Fibonacci extensions.
- Public before/after examples suggest 50% wick fill is often treated as a practical first objective, while 100% wick fill is considered when context still supports continuation.
- Poor risk/reward is a valid reason to skip even when the directional thesis is plausible.

### No-Trade And Anti-Patterns

- Skip when HTF context, liquidity map, trigger, invalidation, or first target is missing.
- Skip mid-range noise unless a separate trigger exists.
- Skip a level touch without reaction; touch is interest, not entry.
- Skip or lower confidence when the trade is crowded, late, or chasing into nearby opposing liquidity.
- Skip event volatility until the first move settles and a second structure appears.
- Use "watchlist only" for interesting levels without trigger.
- Use setup-quality percentages as confluence/read quality, not statistical win probability.

### Session Timing

- Session model: Asia, London, and New York often form repeated patterns. Asia holds then London pumps/dumps and New York reverses; or Asia pumps/dumps, London holds, New York reverses. Avoid applying this blindly on weekends or bank holidays.
- Important invalidation: if London does not hold/range after an Asia move, the New York reversal idea weakens and the next liquidity pool becomes more likely.
- Weekly psychological pattern: weekend range first, Monday false move risk, London confirmation/hold, New York stop-hunt or reversal, then possible midweek reversal if price reclaims the range.

### News or Macro Filters

- Avoid high-impact news days such as CPI/FOMC unless there is a tested plan and risk management. Weekend moves often retrace and can fill CME gaps.
- Around FOMC/CPI, expect false moves and volatility. Early YouTube evidence says he avoids trading shortly before FOMC and for a period after the press conference unless there is a very specific plan.
- CPI-day rule from videos 041-055: do not guess the number or first candle; wait roughly 30-45 minutes after the release, then trade only if price gives a deviation/reclaim/retest setup.
- FOMC-day rule from videos 086-100: rate decision may be priced in; Dot Plot/Powell creates volatility; wait for the event to settle and then trade imbalances left by the move.
- Event-day execution rule from videos 086-095: treat the first event candle as trap/imbalance creation; trade only the second structure after reclaim, retest, rejection, or EMA acceptance clarifies the route.
- Weekend false-move rule from videos 101-115: a large weekend impulse is suspect because liquidity is thin; if it creates a CME gap, expect early-week retrace, then rebuild the map after the gap is filled.

### Why Liquidity Hunting Works (his explicit thesis, videos 138-151 + Week 1 course)

- Price is pulled liquidity-to-liquidity because many exchanges own their own market maker: the exchange holds every trader's stops/liquidations/TPs and can feed the MM enough volume to run price into the densest cluster. The exchange profits from fees and especially liquidations. He cites the FTX/Alameda ruling as precedent.
- Practical rule: before trusting any move, ask where the stops and liquidations sit. Treat the densest cluster as the magnet.
- The MM position mechanic (course Week 1 framing): "To go long, the MM sells you their short. To go short, the MM sells you their long." Every position has a liquidation point; the MM targets those zones because it needs that liquidity to close its own opposing position.
- Flat market → fake move cycle (course Week 1): when price is flat, no one trades and the exchange earns no commissions. So algorithms artificially move price to excite retail into opening positions. **Tell: no noticeable volume on the chart during the move.** These moves are false and get retraced — the imbalance they create is then tradeable.
- OI gaps as magnets: gaps in Open Interest pull price because the market wants as much liquidity triggered as possible. Big OI clusters = potential support/resistance; gaps in OI = price is pulled there to activate resting orders.

### Liquidity Run / Room For Liquidity (videos 138-151)

- A "liquidity run" is a false move one direction to gather liquidity before the real move the other way. Do not fade or chase the first move; trade the move after the sweep.
- Invalidation for "this is a liquidity run": clean acceptance beyond the key level (e.g. acceptance above a round number = real breakout, not a run).

### Liquidity Trend Lines (videos 138-151)

- A clean, obvious ascending trendline with repeated touches builds an ever-larger stop cluster just beneath it; eventually price breaks down to take those stops. Distrust textbook trendlines and expect the sweep below before continuation.

### Cross-Exchange Wick Validation (videos 138-151)

- A wick is only real liquidity if it appears across multiple exchanges (Binance/Bybit/Blofin/BingX/Bitget/KuCoin etc.). Discount a wick that exists on only one chart (Coinbase often differs).

### Manual Wick-Fill Management (videos 131-151)

- Never put a hard stop inside the fill zone ("that's a bad trader"). Place a limit at the 50% level, then judge the reaction: a bounce confirms; consolidation at the 50% level signals continuation to the 100% fill. Invalidation is behavioral, not a fixed stop.
- A wick created only to fill a prior wick is not a new valid imbalance. Greater-than-50% fill means the imbalance can be considered done.

### Execution And Scaling Mechanics (videos 131-151)

- Laddered limit entries: split size across pre-defined levels; the largest, lowest add sits at the highest-confluence point (more confluence = bigger/lower add, not recklessness).
- Add to winners on acceptance; never average into losers. Deploy a final tranche only on acceptance above resistance.
- Preset take-profits at entry (e.g. 66% at weekly 50MA + 33% at HTF resistance; or 1/3 at 1h50MA + 2/3 at 4h50MA). Earlier examples scaled out in 25/50/75% tranches. Move stop to breakeven once price runs.
- Stay flat at mid-range ("no man's land"). Never long right below a HTF resistance (e.g. weekly 50MA); never short against momentum/trend.
- Be flat into known data releases ("uncertainty is not worth trading"). Order-book spoof check: read the book only near the level; vanishing walls = spoof.

### Macro Liquidity Stance (videos 138-151, current)

- Bullish 2026 on a global-liquidity thesis (rising M2, end of QT, expected rate cuts). BTC follows M2 with a ~4-5 month lag.
- Explicitly rejects the four-year cycle and monthly seasonality: "I trade price action because human behavior doesn't change." The assistant should not lean on cycle/seasonality narratives.
- Weekly 50MA remains the bull/bear regime line; a deviation below can be natural, but bear is only confirmed by a weekly close below it followed by a retest as resistance.

## Setup Playbook

### Setup 1: Range Extreme Deviation

- Conditions: price is in a range or consolidation and trades into range high/low or a liquidation cluster.
- Entry trigger: failed acceptance outside the range, return inside, or retest/rejection of the extreme.
- Invalidation: clean acceptance beyond the deviated range extreme.
- Targets: opposite range liquidity, long wick, liquidation pool.
- Best market environment: established range with visible liquidation clusters.
- Bad environment: strong trend acceptance outside the range.
- Evidence: `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-strategy-map.md`

### Setup 2: Wick Fill / Wick Magnet

- Conditions: large unfilled wick or visible liquidity pool.
- Entry trigger: price starts accepting toward the wick, with confluence from EMA/session/liquidation data.
- Invalidation: structure or acceptance moves away from the wick thesis.
- Targets: 50% wick fill, full wick, wick liquidity zone, and nearby liquidation cluster.
- Best market environment: range or retrace where wick remains the obvious liquidity.
- Bad environment: strong opposing trend with no reaction.
- Evidence: `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-strategy-map.md`, `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-001-oldest-videos-2023-04-17-to-2023-04-21.md`

### Setup 3: EMA Supported Reversal After Absorption

- Conditions: price holds or rejects 1h/4h/1W 50 EMA while repeated wicks show no progress.
- Entry trigger: EMA hold/reclaim/rejection plus candle/session confirmation.
- Invalidation: clean progression through the wick/EMA zone.
- Targets: nearest liquidity pool, range extreme, or prior structure.
- Best market environment: compression around a known EMA or range level.
- Bad environment: clean directional acceptance through the level.
- Evidence: `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-strategy-map.md`

### Setup 4: Altcoin HTF Range Preparation

- Conditions: altcoin is in a large high-timeframe range after a bear-market drawdown.
- Entry trigger: not fully defined yet; early videos focus on zones of interest rather than immediate entries.
- Invalidation: do not assume the bottom is in; downside imbalances and range lows remain possible.
- Targets: range midpoint, range high, prior liquidity pools, and unfilled imbalances.
- Best market environment: BTC stable/up and BTC dominance falling.
- Bad environment: BTC falling while BTC dominance rises, because altcoins can make new lows.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-003-videos-011-to-025-2023-04-27-to-2023-05-16.md`

### Setup 5: Full Range Deviation Reversal

- Conditions: price is inside a clear range with obvious liquidity beyond one edge.
- Entry trigger: price sweeps beyond the range, returns inside, then consolidates under resistance or above support.
- Invalidation: acceptance back outside the range edge, especially with strong volume and continuation.
- Targets: mid-range first, opposite range quarter/edge second, then opposite-side liquidity if momentum continues.
- Best market environment: established range, visible liquidation cluster, euphoric breakout or panic breakdown.
- Bad environment: clean high-volume breakout that holds outside the range.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-005-videos-041-to-055-2023-06-08-to-2023-07-18.md`

### Setup 6: Order-Book Liquidity Target

- Conditions: order book, heatmap, or Hyblock shows a large liquidity cluster near a chart level.
- Entry trigger: price loses/reclaims a relevant level such as 1h/4h50EMA, range low/high, range quarter, or trendline in the direction of the cluster.
- Invalidation: liquidity disappears, price accepts away from the target, or DXY/SPX context flips against the route.
- Targets: nearest large liquidity cluster first, then next support/resistance or range level.
- Best market environment: range or slow trend with visible clustered liquidity.
- Bad environment: major news candle before confirmation or clean trend acceptance away from liquidity.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-006-videos-056-to-070-2023-07-19-to-2023-08-11.md`

### Setup 7: Support-Box Sweep To Mid-Range

- Conditions: price trades near an old range low, HTF support, or visually marked support/liquidity box.
- Entry trigger: stop-hunt/sweep into or below the box, then reclaim or hold above it.
- Invalidation: acceptance below the support box or failure to reclaim the marked range level.
- Targets: 1h/4h50EMA, mid-range, daily/weekly 50EMA, range high, or wick-fill target.
- Best market environment: low-liquidity chop, weekly update route, or post-news false move.

### Setup 8: Manipulation Sweep Reversal

- Conditions: obvious round number, range high/low, or breakout level with liquidity beyond it.
- Entry trigger: sweep the level, return back through it, then retest it as resistance/support.
- Invalidation: acceptance beyond the swept level.
- Targets: 15m/1h50EMA, lower/upper wick, liquidity pool, or range midpoint.
- Best market environment: Asia pump, New York volatility, post-news false move, or crowded breakout.
- Bad environment: clean breakout with acceptance and no retest failure.

### Setup 9: 100% Wick-Fill Continuation

- Conditions: 50% wick fill has happened and price does not reject strongly.
- Entry trigger: consolidation above support, range high flip, or 1h/4h50EMA hold after the 50% fill.
- Invalidation: loss of flipped support or EMA.
- Targets: 100% wick fill, then next liquidity pool.
- Best market environment: impulse wick with continued consolidation and liquidity beyond the wick.
- Bad environment: sharp rejection from 50% wick fill.

### Setup 10: News Imbalance After FOMC/CPI

- Conditions: FOMC, CPI, Powell, or similar event creates large candles and visible imbalances.
- Entry trigger: wait until volatility settles, then trade imbalance retrace only with EMA/range/session confirmation.
- Invalidation: price accepts beyond the news impulse without returning.
- Targets: 50%/100% imbalance fill, nearby wick, or session liquidity.
- Best market environment: event volatility that leaves clean liquidity pools after a false move.
- Bad environment: trading before the release, before Powell finishes, or before a reclaim/retest exists.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-008-videos-086-to-100-2023-09-18-to-2024-02-13.md`

### Setup 11: Stop-Loss Zone Sweep

- Conditions: obvious support/resistance with retail stops likely clustered beyond it.
- Entry trigger: sweep into the stop-loss zone, then return below/above the level and retest failure or reclaim.
- Invalidation: acceptance beyond the swept level.
- Targets: opposite side of local range, 1h/4h50EMA, wick fill, or next liquidity pocket.
- Best market environment: clear retail level with heatmap/order-book confluence.
- Bad environment: clean breakout acceptance after the sweep.

### Setup 12: Two-Candle 4h50EMA Gate

- Conditions: 4h50EMA is the active support/resistance decision line.
- Entry trigger: for shorts, close below and test the 4h50EMA as resistance for two 4h candles; for longs, reclaim and hold it as support.
- Invalidation: reclaim against the thesis before confirmation completes.
- Targets: nearest liquidity pool, psychological level, range high/low, or HTF liquidation level.
- Best market environment: post-impulse range where EMA is repeatedly respected.
- Bad environment: strong trend accepting through the EMA without retest.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-009-videos-101-to-115-2024-02-14-to-2024-03-28.md`

- Entry trigger: altcoin reclaim/breakout while BTC.D weakens.
- Invalidation: BTC dumps or BTC.D breaks higher with acceptance.
- Targets: altcoin range high, prior resistance, or sector-relative-strength target.
- Best market environment: BTC strength with delayed altcoin participation.
- Bad environment: BTC falling while dominance rises.

### Setup 14: Indicator-Assisted Liquidity Map

- Conditions: chart has several possible wicks, liquidity pools, sessions, and EMA levels.
- Entry trigger: indicator marks context, but trade needs reclaim, rejection, EMA hold/loss, or range acceptance.
- Invalidation: price accepts through the structural level against the thesis.
- Targets: 50%/100% wick fill, liquidity pool, session liquidity, range level, or EMA reaction zone.
- Best market environment: noisy market where manual context can miss old wicks or liquidity pools.
- Bad environment: using indicator levels without reaction/trigger confirmation.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-010-videos-116-to-130-2024-04-01-to-2024-12-07.md`

### Setup 15: Spot Buying Opportunity

- Conditions: BTC near HTF/range support, ETH/alts showing relative strength, BTC.D not pressuring alts, and a clear buying zone exists.
- Entry trigger: support reaction, reclaim, or consolidation above the spot zone.
- Invalidation: acceptance below the spot zone or BTC/BTC.D context flips against alts.
- Targets: range midpoint/high, prior resistance, or sector-relative-strength target.
- Best market environment: panic/discount at support with improving BTC/ETH/alt structure.
- Bad environment: mid-range chop, BTC breakdown risk, or BTC.D rising aggressively.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-010-videos-116-to-130-2024-04-01-to-2024-12-07.md`

### Setup 16: W-Pattern / Double-Bottom Long

- Conditions: price sweeps equal lows or a liquidity pool below support, often near a 4h50EMA + previous range-low confluence; second leg holds higher.
- Entry trigger: sweep of the lows, strong bullish reaction/wick, reclaim of the broken level or LTF EMA, then the second low holding above the first (W completion).
- Invalidation: acceptance below the second low, or failure to reclaim the level that defines the W.
- Targets: neckline/mid-range first, then range high, prior liquidity pool, or 50%/100% wick fill.
- Best market environment: range low or HTF support with a liquidity sweep and EMA confluence.
- Bad environment: clean breakdown with acceptance below the lows (no reclaim).
- Evidence: `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-weekly-market-update-index.md` (Oct 27 2024; May 26 2024), `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-strategy-map.md` (M/W patterns)

### Setup 17: M-Pattern / Double-Top Short

- Conditions: failed acceptance above a range high or prior high, with liquidity above swept; second high fails lower.
- Entry trigger: sweep above the highs, rejection/failed acceptance, EMA or range loss, second high failing below the first (M completion).
- Invalidation: acceptance above the second high or reclaim of the broken support/EMA.
- Targets: neckline/mid-range first, then range low, nearest liquidation cluster, or wick fill.
- Best market environment: range high, crowded breakout, or distribution after a vertical pump.
- Bad environment: clean breakout with acceptance above the highs and no failure.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-009-videos-101-to-115-2024-02-14-to-2024-03-28.md` (video 106 M-pattern), `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-videos-101-115-trade-chart-patterns.md`

### Setup 18: Liquidity-Run Fade

- Conditions: an obvious move (often into a textbook trendline, round number, or equal highs/lows) that looks like a breakout/breakdown but is into thin or trapped liquidity.
- Entry trigger: let the first move run liquidity, wait for the sweep, then trade the reaction the OTHER way once price returns through the level. Validate the wick across multiple exchanges.
- Invalidation: clean acceptance beyond the key level (then it is a real move, not a run).
- Targets: opposite liquidity pool, 50% imbalance fill, mid-range, or the cluster the run originated from.
- Best market environment: obvious crowded level, liquidity trend line, or pre-news positioning.
- Bad environment: high-volume acceptance through the level with no return.
- Evidence: `sources/first-trader/videos/youtube-batch-012-videos-138-to-151-2025-11-03-to-2026-01-20.md`, `library/first-trader/strategy-notes/crypnuevo-trading-dna.md`

## Trader Type Taxonomy (from Week 1 course)

When a student asks what timeframe to use, apply this:

| Type | Timeframes |
|---|---|
| Scalper | 1m / 5m / 15m / 30m / 1h |
| Day trader | 15m / 30m / 1h / 4h / daily |
| Swing trader | 4h / daily / weekly |
| Position trader | daily / weekly / monthly |

The same setups (liquidity pool, deviation, sweep, retest) appear on every timeframe — from 5m to weekly. The timeframe determines the magnitude, not the method.
