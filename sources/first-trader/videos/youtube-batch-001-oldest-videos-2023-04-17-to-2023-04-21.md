## Clean Summary

These first videos already contain the core CrypNuevo framework seen later on X:

- He starts from a Twitter Sunday update and uses YouTube as daily follow-up / trade-plan management.
- He maps BTC levels from prior ranges, previous resistance/support, long wicks, liquidation heatmaps, DXY, and SPX.
- He treats moves as liquidity runs rather than organic direction by default.
- He repeatedly warns against bias and catching falling knives.
- He prefers scenario trees with invalidation rather than single predictions.
- He treats Asia/London/New York session behavior as a repeatable intraday edge.

## Early Strategy Pattern

### 1. Sunday Update -> Weekday Follow-Up Loop

The first video is explicitly a Monday follow-up to the Sunday Twitter update. This establishes the recurring workflow:

1. Publish Sunday roadmap.
2. Watch whether price triggers the projected move.
3. Use YouTube/X updates to manage the path, target, invalidation, and changing confluence.

This pattern remains central later in the X corpus.

### 2. Liquidity Run / Stop-Hunt Logic

Video 1 identifies BTC moving close to a major liquidation level around 29.1k, bouncing before it, then likely returning to hit stop losses and liquidations. He interprets the bounce as a liquidity run upward to encourage new longs and increase downside liquidity.

Video 2 confirms that Asia took the 29.1k liquidity and reversed. He frames the move as a stop-hunt and reversal, not a random support reaction.

### 3. Market Session Model

Videos 2 and 3 explain the Asia/London/New York pattern:

- Asia pumps or dumps.
- London may hold/range.
- New York may reverse the Asia move.

He does not trade this blindly. If London does not hold, the New York reversal idea is invalidated and he shifts to the next liquidity pool.

### 4. DXY And Macro Intermarket Context

The first video uses DXY as an inverse BTC input:

- DXY sweeping lows and bouncing supports BTC downside.
- DXY approaching resistance/50EMA can create the condition for BTC to bounce.

Videos 4 and 5 add SPX:

- SPX wick fills and psychological levels help shape risk context.
- If SPX loses a key area, BTC can move to deeper HTF support.

### 5. Level-By-Level Decision Tree

Video 3 explicitly says to go level by level, day by day, without bias. Video 4 formalizes this:

- If current support holds and shows repeated downside wicks, a bounce can be considered.
- If support fails, expect price to travel to the next range / next liquidity zone.
- If the next zone breaks, move to the next higher-timeframe support.

### 6. Wick Fill And Liquidation Confluence

Video 4 targets the 50% of a large wick around 27.4k and notes that Hyblock liquidation levels match the same area. Video 5 continues the same target and says liquidation pressure increased as traders tried to catch the bottom.

This is an early version of the later "wick fill strategy":

- Large wick creates imbalance / liquidity.
- 50% wick fill can be a target.
- Liquidation heatmap validates whether the level is attractive.

### 7. EMA Catch-Up / Weekend Caution

Video 5 expects BTC to range over the weekend because liquidity is lower. He watches the 1h50EMA to come lower and potentially be retested before the next move.

Weekend rule:

- Avoid large BTC weekend trades.
- Weekend conditions produce traps, fakeouts, and stop-hunting wicks.
- If trading weekends, he suggests smaller/altcoin-style caution rather than major BTC conviction.
