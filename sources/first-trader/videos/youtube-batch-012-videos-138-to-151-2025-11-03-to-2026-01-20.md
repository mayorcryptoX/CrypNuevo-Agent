## The "WHY" Behind Liquidity Hunting (new — his explicit thesis)

Earlier batches showed *that* he hunts liquidity; this batch explains *why he believes it works* (video 149, restated 151):
- Many exchanges **own their own market-maker company** → conflict of interest. The exchange holds every trader's entries, stop-losses, liquidation levels, and take-profits (we type them into the system) and can pass them to the MM.
- The MM has the volume to push price to the densest stop/liquidation zone.
- The exchange profits from **fees** and especially **liquidations** (a liquidation forfeits the *whole* position — the biggest fee). So moving price into stops/liquidations is directly incentivized.
- He cites the **FTX/Alameda** court finding of manipulation as precedent that this is real and "still happening."
Mentor takeaway: price is pulled **liquidity-to-liquidity**; always ask where the stops/liquidations are before trusting a move.

## New Concepts / Vocabulary

- **"Room for liquidity" / "liquidity run"** — a *false move* in one direction to gather liquidity (hit stops/liquidations) before the *real move* the opposite way, because that makes the real move more efficient. Used constantly (145-151). Do **not** fade or chase the first move blindly; trade the move *after* the liquidity is taken.
- **"Liquidity trend lines"** (video 149) — a clean ascending trendline with repeated support touches builds an **exponentially growing cluster of stop-losses just below it**; eventually price doesn't bounce, it breaks down to take them all. Distrust obvious textbook trendlines.
- **Cross-exchange wick validation** (138/139) — a wick is treated as *real liquidity* only if it appears across Binance, Bybit, Blofin, BingX, Bitget, KuCoin, etc. If it's only on one chart (Coinbase often differs), discount it.
- **Liquidity-to-liquidity trading** (151) — explicitly frames a sequence: hit short liquidations above → reject at weekly 50MA → then go hit the long liquidations below.

## New Tools (not in earlier batches)

- **Options gamma / gamma flush** (142, 150) — large open options force dealers to hedge (sell as price rises, buy as it falls), pinning price in a boring range ("a lid on a bottle"). When a large % of options expire (he cited 66.8% on Dec 26), expect volatility release. He tracks specific call-option strikes (e.g. heavy calls at 90k, then 100k expiring Jan 31) as **range-capping levels** until expiry.
- **Hyblock Capital liquidation data** (142, 146, 151) — he looks at exactly two things:
  1. **Delta** (cumulative long-vs-short liquidation difference). Quantified rule: under ~12B = irrelevant noise; **>20-25-30 billion = strong squeeze/reversal signal**. ("Max liquidity setting; numbers are exaggerated but I've used it 4-5 years.")
  2. **Liquidation levels** — the peaks showing where the most liquidations sit (where the MM would absorb the most). Targets the densest cluster.
- **M2 global liquidity index** (144) — BTC follows global monetary supply with a ~4-5 month lag; rising M2 = unrealized upside (his bull case to >130k).
- **Bitcoin mining cost** (144) — average cost to mine 1 BTC (~74k at the time) acts as a historical price floor; price rarely closes below it except brief deviations (COVID, bear bottoms). Used to argue "the bottom is close."
