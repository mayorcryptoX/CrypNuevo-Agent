## Batch Thesis

This batch is the clearest first-person window yet into *execution mechanics*, *position sizing*, and *risk philosophy* — things earlier batches flagged as unknown. It covers a BTC range (90k-100k) that broke down into a major liquidation cascade (the largest in crypto history at the time, plus the Bybit hack), then a retest of the weekly 50EMA as the line between bull and bear market. Several previously-inferred rules are now stated explicitly by him.

## New / Sharper Rules (not fully captured before)

### 1. Wick-fill must be traded MANUALLY — never with a hard stop in the fill zone
Video 131, verbatim idea: "any trader that was trading that wick-fill strategy with a stop loss in this zone — I'm sorry, that's a bad trader." Invalidation is **manual and behavioral**: place a limit at the 50% level, then watch the reaction. A **bounce** = thesis good. **Consolidation at the 50% level** = signal price continues to the 100% wick. You cannot use a fixed stop because the market maker hits stops then continues. This is the single biggest execution clarification in the batch.

### 2. Concrete position-sizing / staged entries (fills the old "sizing unknown" gap)
Video 132 lays out an actual size plan for the 90k-100k range:
- 50% of size reserved for the 100% wick fill at 90k.
- 20% deployed at a downside liquidity pool (this one triggered); partials taken at 1h50MA and 4h50MA, leaving ~10-12%.
- 30% reserved to deploy only on **acceptance above 100k** — i.e. add to a winning position (compounding), not averaging down.
He splits size across pre-defined levels and only adds the final tranche on confirmation.

### 3. Order-book timing and spoof check
Video 132: do **not** read the order book early. Only open it when price is **near** the level (e.g. 91k-92k for a 90k wall). If big resting buy orders **disappear right before** price arrives, it is spoofing/"puffing" the order — price will pass through. If they stay, they are real support. "The market maker likes to poof the orders."

### 4. Range sub-structure: quarters, and "long every retest"
Video 133: he marks range high, range low, mid-range, **top range quarter, and bottom range quarter**, and longs every retest of a level inside an up-biased range. He does **not** set short limit orders at range highs (front-run risk) — he waits to **observe the rejection**, then trades the move down.

### 5. Weekly 50EMA = the bull/bear line and "safest spot buy"
Videos 135-137: the weekly 50EMA is "historically the best indicator in Bitcoin to tell whether we're in a bull or bear market" and "one of the safest buy areas in a spot in a bull market." Bull pattern he trades: uptrend → retrace → retest weekly 50EMA as support → continuation → new high (cites June 2021, June 2023, Aug 2024). **Bear confirmation** = weekly candle **closes below** the weekly 50EMA and then tests it as resistance for a few weeks (cites Dec 2021). Wicks below are fine if the candle closes back above.

### 6. "2-candle body acceptance" restated for breakouts
Video 134: a level break counts only when a candle **body** (not a wick) breaks it and the **second candle does not recover** the level; from the third candle onward the move continues. Same gate logic seen in the 4h50EMA rule, generalized.

### 7. Liquidation cascades often mark bottoms; don't panic-sell
Videos 134/136: record liquidation cascades historically mark short-term bottoms/bounces (cites Covid, FTX, Luna). Drops of 25%/17%/46%/28% in this cycle were all corrections to the weekly 50EMA before the next leg.

### 8. Macro reads with nuance
- Video 132: the Dec-2024 FOMC drop was a **shakeout, not a turning point** — caused by Powell's "too close to call" comment, while the dot plot was actually bullish (2 cuts vs 1 expected). Distinguishes sentiment shakeouts from fundamental turns.
- Videos 135/137: "no altseason during QT" historically; FOMC minutes hinted QT could end by summer 2025, which "leaves the door open" for alts. QE not required — just the absence of QT.
- DXY up → BTC consolidates/drops (used as a soft filter, "take it with a pinch of salt").

### 9. Honest "I was wrong"
