## Chart Patterns Observed

This batch is one of the clearest "retail trap and liquidity magnet" batches so far. The videos are daily follow-ups during a strong BTC impulse, and he repeatedly refuses to treat price strength alone as an entry.

Visual patterns:

- orange/yellow boxes above resistance are explicitly marked as stop-loss zones;
- white boxes mark resistance/support that retail is likely using for shorts or longs;
- yellow/white paths show sweep high -> return below -> retest -> range/EMA target, or sweep low -> reclaim -> range high/liquidation target;
- the purple 4h50EMA becomes a visible decision line in nearly every BTC chart;
- Hyblock/Bookmap heatmaps are repeatedly checked after the chart route is drawn, especially around 50k, 52k, 53k, and 74.1k;
- M-pattern drawings appear as failed high, second high/sweep, neckline/EMA loss, then liquidity target;
- BTC dominance charts are used as a weekend/altcoin filter rather than a standalone trade trigger;
- video 113 shifts from BTC execution into AI-token theme selection, using strong charts and sector narrative rather than his normal liquidation map.

## Main Strategy Findings

### Stop-Loss Zone Hunting

Videos 101-103 make the stop-loss-zone logic explicit.

Process:

1. Find a very obvious resistance/support that retail can see.
2. Infer where shorts/longs will hide stops.
3. Mark that area as the stop-loss zone.
4. Expect a stop hunt into that zone if heatmap/order-book liquidity agrees.
5. Do not assume continuation after the stop hunt; wait for hold/reclaim or return inside the level.

For upside stop hunts, the trap is:

- clear resistance;
- shorts enter below it;
- stops build above it;
- price pushes into the stop-loss zone;
- if it falls back below resistance, it becomes a short/reversal signal.

For downside stop hunts, the mirror setup is:

- clear support/trendline;
- longs enter above it;
- stops build below it;
- price sweeps below;
- reclaim can trigger upside continuation.

### Strong Resistance Does Not Equal Short

Around 52k, he repeatedly notes that BTC is entering a higher-timeframe resistance zone. The execution rule is not "short resistance."

His decision tree:

- If BTC breaks above resistance and holds/retests as support, he looks for continuation toward 60k or even 65k.
- If BTC spikes above and returns inside, he treats it as a stop hunt/fakeout.
- If BTC ranges below resistance, he expects a rest/consolidation period and trades range extremes rather than forcing a trend call.

This continues his "not bull, not bear" style: a level is a decision point, not a position.

### Two-Candle 4h50EMA Gate

Videos 104 and 107 refine the EMA execution rule into a strict two-candle test.

For shorts:

- price must close below the 4h50EMA;
- then at least two 4h candles should test it as resistance;
- only after that does the next candle carry higher downside probability;
- if price reclaims the 4h50EMA before the resistance test completes, the short trigger is invalidated.

For longs:

- reclaim and support test of the 4h50EMA weakens the bearish case;
- if the EMA continues rising under price, it can become the key support for range-high continuation.

This is one of the most exact execution gates found so far.

### M-Pattern As A Liquidity Distribution Structure

Video 106 makes the M-pattern practical rather than decorative.

The pattern is:

- price ranges under/around a high;
- first high or breakout fails;
- second high/sweep traps late longs or breakout traders;
- price loses the range/EMA area;
- downside target becomes the nearest liquidity pool or psychological level.

He does not trade the letter shape alone. The pattern matters only when it aligns with liquidity, EMA behavior, and failed acceptance above resistance.

### Momentum Filter

Video 110 adds a clear momentum rule.

If price breaks strongly, he avoids shorting immediately into the move even if a liquidity target has been hit. He waits for momentum to fade, usually over several hours, before considering a reversal. When momentum remains strong after a sweep/reclaim, he favors continuation to the next liquidity area.

This explains why he sometimes skips an otherwise valid level: the setup can be right, but timing is wrong if momentum has not cooled.

### Weekend False Move And CME Gap

Video 109 reinforces the weekend/weekly psychological pattern:

- a large weekend move is suspicious because institutional liquidity is thin;
- if a CME gap is created, early-week retrace is likely;
- the retrace of the weekend imbalance is not the false move; the low-liquidity weekend impulse is the false move;
- once the gap is filled, he rebuilds the map from the current range/liquidity context.

This is important because he does not blindly keep fading after the gap is filled. He reassesses whether range highs, range lows, 50k, or 53k have the better liquidity magnet.

### BTC Dominance Fade And Altcoin Catch-Up

Videos 108 and 111 show the altcoin logic in this stage.

Altcoin long interest improves when:

- BTC is strong or stable rather than dumping;
- BTC dominance is at a resistance/fade area or starts rejecting;
- alts have not yet caught up to BTC's move;
- BTC is not sitting at an immediate breakdown trigger.

This is conditional. He is not buying alts only because BTC.D is high. He wants BTC structure to avoid killing the alt trade.

### Upside Liquidation Route

Videos 112, 114, and 115 focus on upside liquidation pockets.

Common route:

- price holds or reclaims a support box;
- lower-timeframe structure forms higher lows;
- momentum points up;
- heatmap/order-book shows upside liquidity;
- target becomes the next liquidity pocket, not a random round number.

Video 115 gives the strongest HTF example: 74.1k is treated as an upside liquidation magnet after BTC holds the support/reclaim path. The route still requires lower-timeframe acceptance; the HTF target does not justify chasing if structure fails.

### AI Token Video

Video 113 is less useful for BTC execution but useful for his sector-selection thinking.

Observed process:

- identify a strong crypto narrative, here AI;
- look for tokens with strong charts and relative strength;
- compare exchange availability, liquidity, and chart structure;
- treat the theme as a watchlist/narrative filter, not the same as a BTC day-trade trigger.

This is a separate playbook branch: narrative/sector selection rather than liquidity-map scalping.

## Exact Setup Library Additions

### Setup: Stop-Loss Zone Sweep

- Conditions: obvious support/resistance with retail stops likely clustered beyond it.
- Entry trigger: sweep into stop-loss zone, then return below/above the level and retest failure or reclaim.
- Targets: opposite side of local range, 1h/4h50EMA, wick fill, or next liquidity pocket.
- Invalidation: acceptance beyond the swept level.

### Setup: Two-Candle 4h50EMA Gate

- Conditions: 4h50EMA is the active support/resistance decision line.
- Short trigger: close below 4h50EMA, then two candles test it as resistance.
- Long trigger: reclaim and hold/test the 4h50EMA as support.
- Targets: nearest liquidity pool, 50k/psychological level, range high/low, or HTF liquidation level.
- Invalidation: reclaim against the thesis before the two-candle test completes.

### Setup: Momentum Continuation After Sweep

- Conditions: price sweeps support/resistance and reclaims with strong momentum.
- Entry trigger: hold/retest after the sweep while momentum remains strong.
- Targets: range high/low, next liquidation pocket, or HTF level.
- Invalidation: momentum fades and price returns back through the reclaim level.

### Setup: BTC.D Fade For Altcoin Catch-Up

- Conditions: BTC is stable/up, BTC dominance reaches resistance or begins rejecting, and alts lag BTC.
- Entry trigger: altcoin reclaims local support or breaks out while BTC.D weakens.
- Targets: altcoin range high, prior resistance, or sector-relative strength target.
- Invalidation: BTC dumps or BTC.D breaks higher with acceptance.

The main change is precision. Earlier batches already had liquidity, wick fill, sessions, and EMAs. This batch adds exact trigger patience: two EMA tests, momentum cooling, support/reclaim confirmation, and no blind trade at resistance.
