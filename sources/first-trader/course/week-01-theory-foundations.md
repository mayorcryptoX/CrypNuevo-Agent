## Key Statements (verbatim or close paraphrase)

### Why Most People Lose

> "Most people lose money in crypto because they don't know anything about Market Psychology and sadly, they get trapped and then liquidated."

> "Conventional TA assumes that price always moves in a direction based on fixed patterns and ignores Market Psychology. That's why traders are caught in fake outs or triangles break in the opposite direction as expected."

> "Human behavior doesn't change. That's why MM exploits the retail emotions. Most traders lose money, so why would you then follow the herd and the market sentiment?"

### Market Maker Mechanics (explicit course framing)

> "A market maker is an individual participant or member firm of an exchange that provides liquidity. They are high-volume traders that literally 'make a market'. They're basically liquidity providers run by algorithms."

> "Contrary to what most people think, when you trade a liquid asset, you don't make money because somebody else is losing. You're not buying someone's sell order. Your order is matched almost instantly to a liquidity provider AKA Market Maker."

**The position mechanic (exact course framing):**
- To go long → MM sells you their short
- To go short → MM sells you their long

> "Behind every trading position there is a liquidation point and the idea is that the MM is going to try to take those zones later on because he has given you the liquidity to open the trade."

> "When your long is in a profit, where are you getting the liquidity from to close it at a profit? From the MM because the order that is against you, it is a short."

> "They will move price in whatever way they want to wherever the most liquidity lies by providing money to induce traders to think something is happening when the opposite is."

> "They never buy high and sell low so when prices are rising we could say that MM are building their shorts and when price is dropping MM are building their longs."

### Exchange Business Model → Flat Market → Fake Moves

> "Exchanges make money through trading commissions and especially with liquidations. They make money with trading fees when you open a position and also, when you close it."

> "Imagine that the price of an asset is flat and not moving at all for some time. The traders are bored so they don't want to put money in. Is the exchange making any money during this time? No, because they make money from the trading commissions from our trades."

> "So this is when the algorithms (MM) can manipulate the price. They will move price (and you'll notice because there won't be any noticeable volume in the chart) so that the retail trader can get a bit excited and open a trade so the market gains liquidity and the exchange can make money. These moves tend to be false moves and get retraced so we can take advantage of them and trade the imbalances."

### Liquidity Definition

> "When we talk about liquidity, we talk about: the current open positions (Open Interest), liquidations, stop losses, & buy/sell limit orders that reflect all the activity in the market."

> "All the buy and sell orders show up in the orderbook. Stop losses and liquidations are also buy/sell orders, so they also show."

### Open Interest

> "Big clusters of Open Interest can give us an idea of potential supports and resistances."

> "Gaps in Open Interest act as magnetic zones for price. Why? Because the market wants as much liquidity in play as possible so they'll go to those gap areas to trigger orders in that zone."

### Liquidations

> "Targeting traders' liquidations is probably the most efficient move for the market maker, as it would release a large amount of liquidity."

Platforms mentioned for tracking liquidations: Hyblock Capital, TheKingFisher, Vesco.

### Stop Losses — How He Places Them Differently

> "Most traders place their stop losses and open positions in the same areas because most traders trade in the same way. So the algorithms are not only hitting the stop loss or liquidation points of one trader, but of a large number of traders which is a big amount of liquidity."

> "When a stop is hit, this effectively removes traders from the market, releasing their liquidity. Market makers have a good idea of where stop losses will be placed, consequently they can then hit them and proceed to use the liquidity to move price."

Key teaching: **Do not place SL closely above resistance or below support** — those are common targets. The course explicitly shows the typical retail SL placement (tight, at the obvious level) vs. CrypNuevo's approach (wider, outside the sweep zone, after a sweep-and-return entry).

> "That's why we can usually see a sweep of the highs / a sweep of the lows before a reversal."

### Breakout Traps (Limit Orders)

> "This isn't a free market. It's a business and this is about taking money from one pocket to the next, by providing liquidity to make traders think something is happening when the opposite is."

> "Conventional fixed patterns are an excuse to trap traders and hunt for liquidity. MMs are out to liquidate people and take their money. This is why, in my opinion, any trader should be very cautious, or even stop trading breakouts of fixed patterns such as triangles and start learning Price Action and data analysis to figure out where the liquidity is."

> "The more liquidity, the better for the market; therefore, the Market Maker will always try to push price towards the nearest and largest liquidity zones."

> "We can see the MM's footprints and intentions in the candlesticks." (Candlesticks taught Week 2.)

### Market-Agnostic + Timeframe Universality

> "We see the same setups playing out in a 5min time frame, 15min, 1h, 4h, daily or weekly time frame."
